Motivation

At fixed mass, concentrations scatter by ~0.1 dex — and the scatter carries assembly information

02 / 19
Where it matters for clusters
  • Weak-lensing masses: c is degenerate with M in the inner profile
  • Secondary properties at fixed mass: relaxation, magnitude gap, BCG dominance, centring
  • Mocks and semi-analytic models need c per halo, not a median c(M)
  • Low-mass concentrations constrain warm dark matter
What existed

  • Ludlow+14, 16: inner density ∝ ρcrit when the collapsed-mass history reached M−2; individual haloes, tested to z ≤ 2

  • Zhang+25: neural net on full MAHs; RMSE 0.085 → 0.129 dex from z = 0 → 2

The clock · 1

The excursion-set clock: each mass element performs a random walk in δ as the smoothing scale shrinks

03 / 19
Definitions

Mass variance   S(M) ≡ σ²(M): variance of the z = 0 linear field in a top-hat of radius R = (3M/4πρ̄m)1/3; decreases with M

Growth   D(z), normalised to D(0) = 1

Collapse threshold   δsc(z) ≡ 1.686 / D(z): the spherical-collapse δc expressed in units of today's linear field; it rises with z

Peak height   ν = δsc(z) / σ(M)

Reading the walk
  • Smoothing on smaller scales (larger S) makes δ0(S) a random walk; Markovian for a sharp-k filter
  • First crossing of δsc(z) at S(M) → the point sits in a halo of mass M at z
  • Raising the barrier to δsc(z′ > z) gives the main progenitor mass at z′
Illustrative walk. Numbers for Mvir = 2.8×1014 M⊙ at z = 0.35 (Planck 2018).
Wang, Lin, Zhu & Luan 2026 · arXiv:2611.17676Journal club · IAG/USP
Define everything: S = sigma^2 at z=0, D normalised at z=0, delta_sc = 1.686/D is the collapse barrier in units of today's field, so it works as a time coordinate. The walk: first crossing of the barrier at S(M) gives the halo mass; a higher barrier (earlier time) is first crossed at larger S, i.e. smaller mass: the main progenitor.
The clock · 2

ωf: formation time measured in units of the walk's own step between M/2 and M

04 / 19
ωf = δsc(zf) − δsc(z)[ S(fM) − S(M) ]1/2
Ingredients
  • M: halo mass at the redshift z where it is observed (Mvir or M200c)
  • zf: highest redshift at which the main progenitor already holds ≥ fM; here f = 1/2 (half-mass time). For f ≥ 1/2 at most one progenitor can qualify, so it is unique. Measured on each simulated halo's main branch
  • Numerator: how far the barrier rises between observation and formation, in units of δc
  • Denominator: r.m.s. step of the walk between M and fM; Gaussian and history-independent for Markovian walks
Worked example — a cluster
M200m = 3×1014 M⊙ at z = 0.35Mvir = 2.8×1014
σ(M), σ(M/2)0.806, 0.939
√[S(M/2) − S(M)]0.482
D(0.35) → δsc(0.35)0.832 → 2.027
half mass in place at zf = 0.92 → δsc2.675
ωf = (2.675 − 2.027) / 0.4821.34
16–84% of P(ωf): 0.82–1.97 ↔ zf0.71–1.17
Why it is useful

The first-passage problem then depends on (zf, z, M, P(k)) only through ωf (Lacey & Cole 1993): p(ωf) = 2ωf erfc(ωf/21/2), mode 0.75, mean 1.06, nearly universal. Large ωf = early formation.

Wang, Lin, Zhu & Luan 2026 · arXiv:2611.17676Journal club · IAG/USP
Key definitions: z_f is the half-mass time of the main progenitor; M is the mass at the observation redshift. Numerator = barrier distance (time in units of delta_c), denominator = rms walk step between M/2 and M. Universality of p(omega) is why it is a good clock. Worked example: a typical DES-like cluster; omega = 1.34 is the median of the simulated distribution.

Model

The model: four regressors, one linear fit in log c

08 / 19
log cΔ = A + αD log D(z) + ασ log σ(M) + β log(1 + ωf) + γ log Ahm
D(z) · background

Linear growth factor, D(0) = 1. Enters halo statistics through δsc = 1.686/D

σ(M) · mass scale

z = 0 linear r.m.s.; real-space top-hat for CDM, sharp-k filter for WDM (avoids the top-hat's k−3 leakage)

ωf · assembly

Half-mass formation variable of this halo, from its main branch (previous slide)

Ahm · WDM cutoff

Ahm = 1 + a Mhm/MΔ, Mhm = (4π/3) ρ̄ (π/khm)³, with TWDM/TCDM = 1/2 at khm; a fixed from the peak model; Ahm = 1 for CDM

Fit

Ordinary least squares on all simulations and snapshots z = 0–5 pooled, separately for {relaxed, all} × {Δvir (Bryan & Norman), 200c}. If αD = ασ, the first two terms collapse to −α log ν: concentration would be a function of peak height alone.

Model

Why the growth factor and not (1+z)? Because time enters the excursion set only through D

09 / 19
The argument
  • The linear field at z is D(z) × today's: redshift enters only via δsc(z) = 1.686/D(z), and mass and time combine as D(z)σ(M) = σ(M, z)
  • In EdS with power-law P(k) the only scale is M*(z), so c = f(M/M*) = f(ν)
  • Writing D instead of (1+z) makes one set of coefficients usable across cosmologies
But the fit gives αD ≠ ασ

1.03 vs 0.66 (Δvir), 0.79 vs 0.64 (200c): extra redshift dependence beyond ν. Physically plausible, since rΔ is defined against ρcrit(z) or Δvir(z) — the expansion history, not growth (pseudo-evolution; Diemer, More & Kravtsov 2013). D is a proxy for it only in ΛCDM → backup B2

Warm dark matter

One extra term, tied to the half-mode mass, carries the WDM suppression

Ingredients
  • khm: where the WDM transfer function is half the CDM one; Mhm = (4π/3) ρ̄ (π/khm)³

Claims
  • Global coefficients predict ΛWDM haloes with ≈ 0.09 dex scatter, no tilt with z

What to ask

Conclusions as stated by the authors

Backup · B2

B2 · Separate D and σ slopes break scale-free self-similarity

BACKUP B2
Argument
  • With ν = δc/(Dσ): log c = A′ − αD log ν + (ασ − αD) log σ + …: extra σ (≡ D) dependence at fixed ν
  • Scale-free EdS (P ∝ k−2): only scale M*(t); vir, 200c, 200m are fixed contrasts; ωf = g(ν, a/af) → c = f(ν, ωf), i.e. αD = ασ
  • Fit predicts −0.29 dex (vir), −0.12 dex (200c) by z = 5 at fixed ν: forbidden
  • SCDM cannot decide: σ(M) also tracks neff, so F(ν, σ) is allowed
  • ΛCDM: extra z dependence is real (Ωm(z), Δvir(z), pseudo-evolution); D is only a proxy
Test and fix
  • Plot log c residuals vs z at fixed ν in the scale-free run
  • Pooled sample: high z only in small boxes → log D, log σ nearly collinear
  • Fix: ν for growth, plus a term constant in EdS, e.g. log Ωm(z): degenerate with αD log D in ΛCDM, differs outside it

Self-similarity is a symmetry of power-law EdS, not a modelling assumption: extra variables are fine if they are scale-free (nu, omega_f) or carry a real scale (M_hm, n_eff, Omega_m(z)). alpha_D != alpha_sigma is a dependence on absolute time at fixed nu, which has nothing to attach to in the scale-free run: 0.29 dex drift predicted, physically forbidden. SCDM is not a clean test because sigma also encodes spectral shape. In LCDM the extra z dependence is real but should live on Omega_m(z) or the overdensity definition, not on D.

B7 · Connection to our joint-fit paper: ωf as the latent cause behind γcm and δMλ

BACKUP B7
Vitorelli & Cypriano 2026 (arXiv:2610.26492)
  • Joint per-cluster fit of WaZP + DES Y3: log c = log c0 + αcm log M + βcm log(1+z) + γcm Δm
  • Gap only in c: γcm = 0.057+0.020−0.026 mag−1
  • With δMλΔm in the mass–richness relation: γcm = 0.007 ± 0.033, δMλ = 0.035+0.016−0.012 dex mag−1
What this paper adds
  • Assembly rank a ≡ Φ−1[F(ωf | M, z)] ⊥ M; sc = β σx ≈ 0.07 dex per unit rank
  • If the gap acts on c only through assembly: γcm = sc ρ / σΔm ≤ 0.07/0.50 ≈ 0.14 mag−1
  • Measured 0.057 ⇒ ρ(Δm, assembly) ≈ 0.4: a loose clock (Dariush+10; Raouf+14)
  • Paper-1 mocks injected 0.167: above that ceiling
Richness is a collider
  • λobs ← M, assembly (satellite survival), orientation, projection; conditioning on λobs (selection, mass assignment) links them
  • Linear toy: assembly → richness alone gives δMλ ≈ 0.033 and a spurious γcm ≈ 0.035, with no gap effect on c or M
  • On our 6-bin grid a mass error leaks into c with κ ≈ 1.0–1.1 ⇒ 0.036–0.040 drop when δMλ is freed; observed 0.050
  • Next: causal simulations with destacking + xlensing
Conditioning on λobs (selection, mass assignment) opens M ↔ a ↔ orientation/projection: the gap then predicts mass at fixed richness without causing it.
